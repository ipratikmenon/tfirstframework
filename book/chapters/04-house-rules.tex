\chapter{House Rules: The Verification Discipline}
\label{ch:house-rules}

Most of the rules in this chapter were written after something went wrong.
That is why they are worth reading: a rule invented in advance records what
somebody thought would matter, and a rule written after a failure records what
actually did. This chapter states each rule, and states the failure it was a
reaction to.

The chapter exists because of a single episode. In \Sn{29} the programme
announced that it had proved the Clay Prize problem for \(u_0\in H^1(\T^3)\)
and began preparing an \texttt{arXiv} submission. In \Sn{31} an independent
line-by-line audit found eight defects in the argument, three of them
elementary --- an arithmetically impossible induction, an invalid H\"older
triple, a reversed Jensen inequality --- and the claim was withdrawn.
Chapter~\ref{ch:audit} is the audit. This chapter is what the programme did
about it.

\section{The numerical rules}
\label{sec:numerical-rules}

\subsection{Method}

Spectral methods only, FFT-based; finite differences are not acceptable.
Dealiasing by the \(2/3\) rule on every run. RK4 time integration throughout,
with an adaptive CFL step. In Route~1, \(\rho=\mathrm{const}\) and
\(\nabla\cdot u=0\) are enforced \emph{exactly} --- by a spectral Leray
projection --- not approximately.

The rationale is that the quantities the programme measures are
high-derivative, small-scale objects: \(\abs{\nabla\ehat}^2\),
\(\abs{\nabla^2\ehat}^2\), palinstrophy. A method with numerical dissipation
at the grid scale would manufacture exactly the smoothness the programme is
looking for. Spectral accuracy makes the observed smoothness attributable to
the equations.

Exactness of the divergence constraint is not pedantry either. \Sn{08}
found a genuine bug in the Leray projector arising from the Nyquist mode:
\texttt{numpy.fft.fftfreq} places the Nyquist frequency at \(-N/2\), so the
mode and its conjugate partner land at the same index, the Hermitian symmetry
required for a real inverse transform fails on the Nyquist planes, and the
divergence escapes --- to an RMS of \(1.465\) at \(N=16\). Zeroing the three
Nyquist planes before the projection fixed it, and the divergence dropped to
below \(10^{-11}\). The programme's reported divergence residuals thereafter
are \(9.85\times10^{-16}\) (NumPy, float64) and \(5.16\times10^{-7}\) (JAX,
float32) on the Taylor--Green benchmark.

\subsection{Recording}

\begin{record}
\textbf{Rule.} Every run saves a parameters JSON, its raw output, its figures
and its \textsf{PASS}/\textsf{FAIL} verdict. Random seeds are always set and
logged. Grid resolution is always logged. Results are appended to
\texttt{results/results.db}. \emph{A result without a logged
\texttt{claim\_id} is not a result}, and \textsf{PASS} may not be claimed
without a logged \texttt{key\_metric}.
\end{record}

The programme broke this rule once, and the breach is instructive. \Sn{30}
reports two experiments, \texttt{EXP-L4-SIGMA-TG-001} and
\texttt{EXP-L4-SIGMA-SH-001}, with \(\sigma\) values and a verdict --- but
they were written to a Markdown file, \texttt{results/S30\_sigma\_results.md},
and never to the database. They are consequently not in the thirty-one tables,
cannot be queried, and cannot be cross-checked against a later run. The
\(\sigma\)-framework they supported is the material the \Sn{31} audit hit
hardest.

\section{The verification rules}
\label{sec:verification-rules}

\subsection{Worktree isolation and independent re-derivation}

This is the strongest rule the programme has, and it was established in
\Sn{41}.

\begin{record}
\textbf{Rule (the merge gate).} Analytical work delegated to a subagent is
performed in an isolated \texttt{git} worktree, not against the shared proof
document. Nothing merges until the orchestrator has \emph{independently
re-derived the load-bearing computations by hand}. A self-report, however
carefully written, is not verification.
\end{record}

Figure~\ref{fig:merge-gate} shows the gate. Its value is not hypothetical. In
\Sn{44} a subagent returned a favourable-sounding headline: that the
band-absorption conjecture was strictly \emph{weaker} than the \(c_K\)
decorrelation conjecture, needing only an \(O(1)\) constant where \(c_K\)
needs a decaying one, so that the programme's four sessions of measurements at
\(c_K\approx1\) already supported it. Verification found the supporting lemma
inverted: it stated
\[
  \Xi[\mathbf 1_E] \;=\; c_K\,\frac{\abs{E}}{\abs{Q(\rstar)}} \;\le\; c_K
  \qquad\text{(wrong)},
\]
whereas the correct relation carries the reciprocal volume factor,
\[
  \Xi[\mathbf 1_E] \;=\; c_K\,\frac{\abs{Q(\rstar)}}{\abs{E}} \;\ge\; c_K .
\]
Three conclusions drawn from the inverted form were withdrawn
(\texttt{rem:s44-corrigendum}, line~8708), and the corrected reading
\emph{inverts the headline}: the volume-normalised statistic is stronger, not
weaker, and the logarithm is not an artefact. Had the inverted lemma merged,
it would have entered the document as good news and steered later sessions
toward the wrong target.

The checks the orchestrator ran that located it are worth naming, because they
are cheap and general: re-derive the algebra directly from the definitions; run
a numerical check on a handful of random configurations (the stated form
matched nothing, the corrected form matched to machine precision); and test
the sanity case (constant densities on \(E\) give \(c_K=1\), uncorrelated, but
\(\Xi=\abs{Q}/\abs{E}\gg1\)). The error also contradicted two of the
subagent's own results elsewhere in the same submission, and that internal
inconsistency is what drew attention to it.

\subsection{The proof document governs}

Where \texttt{PROGRESS.md}, a handover file or a session log disagrees with
\texttt{proofs/}\allowbreak\texttt{claim\_a\_3d\_}\allowbreak\texttt{proof\_attempt.tex}, the document wins and the
disagreement is recorded. \Sn{47} applied this rule to its own commissioning
brief and caught three overstatements, all in work produced one session
earlier. They are set out in \S\ref{sec:source-hierarchy} of
the opening chapter, \emph{How to Read This Book}; the material one is that the \Sn{31} audit has
\emph{eight} grounds, not seven, and the omitted ground is the first
appearance of the programme's central obstruction.

\subsection{Compile; do not grep}

Until \Sn{47}, the proof document had \emph{never been compiled}. Every prior
``verification'' was brace counting by \texttt{grep}. \Sn{39} discovered that
\texttt{conjecture} and \texttt{fact} had been used as environments across
several sessions without ever being declared, so the file would have failed on
first compile --- an error class that brace counting cannot see by
construction. \Sn{47} installed a toolchain and compiled: \(142\) pages, and
three further defects that only a compiler finds. \(\backslash\)\texttt{fint}
and \(\backslash\)\texttt{colonequals} were undefined, which meant three
formulas \emph{were not typesetting at all}.

Two toolchain facts are recorded in the programme's rules because each costs
an hour:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \texttt{cm-super} must be installed, or \texttt{microtype}'s font
  expansion aborts the run with ``auto expansion is only possible with
  scalable fonts'' and the toolchain looks broken when it is not.
\item \texttt{-file-line-error} reformats \TeX{} errors as
  \texttt{file:line:} with \textbf{no leading} \texttt{!}. Grepping for
  \verb|^!| silently reports zero errors on a run that has five. This actually
  happened in \Sn{47}.
\end{enumerate}

The structural checker \texttt{scripts/check\_proof.py} was written in the
same session --- brace balance, \(\backslash\)\texttt{begin}/%
\(\backslash\)\texttt{end} multiset \emph{and nesting order via a stack},
undeclared environments, duplicate labels, unresolved references, and the
malformed \verb|\end{env>| typo class found in \Sn{44}. Its docstring states
explicitly that green is \emph{necessary but not sufficient}: it cannot see
undefined macros, which is precisely how the three \Sn{47} defects survived
every previous check.

\subsection{Never guess a cross-reference}

\Sn{47} resolved four dangling references. The named trap is that a guessed
target injects a false cross-reference into a mathematical document, so each
resolution had to be justified by evidence and each site now carries a comment
recording it --- for instance, \texttt{thm:vorticity-uniform} resolved to
\texttt{thm:uniform-enstrophy} because the citing line quotes that theorem's
displayed conclusion, \(Z(r,z_0)\le D\,r^{2/3}\), verbatim. Where no
identification is possible, the reference must be \emph{declared} dangling
with a marker and a visible note in the body, never guessed.

\section{The reporting rules}
\label{sec:reporting-rules}

\subsection{A negative result is reported as negative}

The book's named failure mode is narrative drift into self-defence: softening
``proved impossible'', upgrading ``conditional'' to ``proved'', describing an
unfavourable measurement as inconclusive but promising. Three episodes set
the standard.

\Sn{45} measured the quantity on which one of the two remaining closure routes
depends --- the \(\Lfac\)-dependence of the correlation constant \(c_K\) ---
and found the regression slope of \(\log c_K\) on \(\log\Lfac\) to be
\(-0.196\) with \(r^2=0.05\) on one pool and \(+0.088\) with \(r^2=0.02\)
across all twelve runs, against the \(-1\) a clean \(C/\Lfac\) law requires,
over \(\Lfac\in[1.35,4.68]\). The session log states the finding as a negative
signal and states, in the same paragraph, both that it does not refute the
conjecture and that the programme had by then been looking at \(c_K\approx1\)
for four sessions without ever seeing the decay it needs.

\Sn{41} was commissioned to close the last mechanical step in a chain and
returned a proof that the step \emph{cannot} be closed by the proposed method,
for any choice of parameters. The result is logged as the main finding of the
session, and the item it concerns was reclassified from ``open-mechanical'' to
genuinely open --- a strictly worse position than the programme believed it
was in, honestly recorded.

\Sn{47} classified the Calder\'on--Zygmund logarithm as \OPEN{} rather than
\IMPOSSIBLE{}, on the ground that no proof in the document says it cannot be
removed. Recording it as a proved barrier would have overstated it in the
programme's own favour --- it would have made the wall sound like a discovery
rather than a difficulty.

\subsection{Specification errors are named as loudly as mathematical ones}

In \Sn{39} a numerical verification track returned two runs with margins of
\(5.5\times10^{19}\) and \(2.0\times10^{14}\), reported as \textsf{PARTIAL}.
The subsequent addendum states flatly that the original \emph{specification}
was wrong and the runs were vacuous: accumulating \(\exp\int_0^T A\,ds\) over
a twenty-time-unit window with \(A\sim O(1\text{--}3.5)\) gives \(e^{70}\),
so the margin could not have been satisfied by any residual, however small.
The addendum names this as an error in the coordinator's specification, not in
the implementation, ``because the programme's convention is that spec errors
get named as explicitly as mathematical ones''. The windowed re-run that
followed reduced the Gr\"onwall factor from \(\sim10^{12}\) to \(O(3\text{--}5)\)
and produced informative comparisons.

\subsection{Self-audit before building on your own work}

\Sn{34} opens with a corrigendum to \Sn{33} --- a dimensional re-audit of the
previous session's own section, performed \emph{before} building on it, which
found and fixed four defects. \Sn{36} corrects a ``bonus'' good term claimed in
\Sn{35}, showing it cancels exactly (\texttt{lem:w2-cancel}, line~6166).
\Sn{37} catches its own first-pass assumption that the global enstrophy is
\(O(1)\) near a putative singularity, which is false, and redoes the
derivation. \Sn{43} corrects \Sn{42}'s reading of a set of numerical spikes,
finding that ``likely resolution artefacts'' was half wrong --- the spikes grew
under refinement --- and replaces it with the accurate characterisation.

\begin{obsv}[What the discipline did and did not buy]
\label{obs:discipline-limits}
The discipline of this chapter caught, in the sessions after it was installed:
an inverted lemma (\Sn{44}), a retracted good term (\Sn{36}), a wrong
enstrophy assumption (\Sn{37}), a wrong reading of numerical spikes
(\Sn{43}), three summary overstatements and three non-typesetting formulas
(\Sn{47}), and a vacuous specification (\Sn{39}). It did not produce a single
new theorem. Verification discipline is a way of not being wrong; it is not a
way of being right.
\end{obsv}
