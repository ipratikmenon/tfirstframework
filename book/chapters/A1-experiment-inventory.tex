\chapter{Experiment Inventory}
\label{ch:experiments}

\begin{record}
\textbf{Source and method.} Every row below was read from
\texttt{results/results.db} by direct query. Nothing here is reconstructed
from a session log or a summary; where a log and the database disagree, the
database governs, and the disagreement is footnoted.

\smallskip
The database has \(31\) tables. Twenty-one of them are \emph{summary} tables,
one row per experiment, carrying \texttt{exp\_id}, \texttt{claim\_id},
\texttt{key\_metric} and \texttt{verdict}; nine are \emph{timeseries} tables
recording per-snapshot traces; one (\texttt{sqlite\_sequence}) is
\texttt{SQLite}'s own bookkeeping. The twenty-one summary tables hold
\textbf{124 experiments} in total.
\end{record}

\section{The identifier convention}
\label{sec:a1-ids}

Experiment identifiers follow \texttt{EXP-L\{layer\}-\{route\}-\{tag\}-\{nnn\}}:
\texttt{EXP-\allowbreak{}L2-\allowbreak{}R1-\allowbreak{}CCF-\allowbreak{}003} is Layer~2, Route~1, CCF profile, third run;
\texttt{EXP-\allowbreak{}L4-\allowbreak{}BRIDGE-\allowbreak{}ADV-\allowbreak{}002} is Layer~4, the bridge diagnostic, adversarial
initial condition, second run. Layer-4 identifiers name the diagnostic rather
than the route, because from \Sn{32} onward every Layer-4 module serves the
alignment programme and the route label stopped discriminating.

The house rule is that a result without a logged \texttt{claim\_id} is not a
result, and that \textsf{PASS} may not be claimed without a logged
\texttt{key\_metric}. Both are honoured almost everywhere. The exceptions are
enumerated in \S\ref{sec:a1-exceptions} rather than hidden.

\section{Layer 2 --- the two-dimensional solvers}
\label{sec:a1-l2}

The lambda sweep is the M3 test of Conjecture~3.5, run at the six
Wang et al.\ values plus an adversarial one. Every run returns the same
\(A_{\min}=3.0801\times10^{-5}\), which is not a coincidence and not a bug:
\(A_{\min}\) is set by the background temperature through the property engine,
not by the flow. The suppression ratio falls monotonically with \(\lambda\)
and no threshold \(\lambda_c\) appears. The MULIMIT series is the Phase-4
\(\mu(T)\to\nu\) limit, with \(\abs{\varepsilon(\delta)}\) falling linearly in
\(\delta\) across four decades.

\section{Layer 3 --- the three-dimensional solvers}
\label{sec:a1-l3}

Taylor--Green is the validation benchmark: energy ratio \(0.9999\),
\(E_0\) error \(0.00\%\), divergence RMS \(9.85\times10^{-16}\) in NumPy and
\(5.16\times10^{-7}\) in JAX float32. The Route-2 series carries the
programme's most-repeated finding --- \(\delta_{\max}\) is unchanged across
the whole \(\varepsilon\) sweep including \(\varepsilon=0\), i.e.\ on the exact
Prize equations. The M9 blow-up search found no blow-up at \(N=64\), \(128\)
or \(256\), with \(Z_{\max}/Z_0\approx0.982\) --- enstrophy \emph{decreasing}
throughout.

The one \textsf{FAIL} in the whole database is
\texttt{EXP-\allowbreak{}L3-\allowbreak{}R2-\allowbreak{}ANISO0050-\allowbreak{}JAX-\allowbreak{}128}. It is discussed in
Chapter~\ref{ch:numerical-lessons}, \S\ref{sec:resolution}: the configuration
is pinned to the module's hard grid floor, its axial extent representable as
one grid cell, and the controlled comparison at aspect ratio \(0.1\) passes at
both resolutions.

\begin{obsv}[What the inventory shows about verdicts]
\label{obs:a1-verdicts}
Of \(124\) logged experiments, \(118\) are \textsf{PASS}, four are
\textsf{PARTIAL}, one is \textsf{FAIL}, and one carries a sentence instead of
a verdict. A pass rate of \(95\%\) should not be read as success. It is a
consequence of what the verdicts \emph{are}: nearly every one is a check that
the solver conserved what it should, that \(A_{\min}\) stayed positive, that
\(\delta\) stayed non-negative, or that a diagnostic's median stayed below a
threshold chosen as ``\(O(1)\) or below''. Those are instrument checks, and an
instrument that fails them is broken.

\smallskip
The one \textsf{FAIL} is a grid artefact. The four \textsf{PARTIAL}s are a
specification error and its repair. The single row that ever contradicted a
programme claim is the one whose verdict field is a sentence --- and its
reading, correctly, is that it tested the wrong equation.

\smallskip
A numerical programme whose verdicts almost never fail is not being asked
questions that could come out either way. That is the same finding as
Chapter~\ref{ch:numerical-lessons}'s, arrived at by counting.
\end{obsv}
