\subsection{The Prize Equations}

The 3D incompressible Navier--Stokes equations with constant viscosity $\nu > 0$:
\begin{align}
  \partial_t \mathbf{u} + (\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}
    &= -\nabla p + \nu\,\Delta\mathbf{u}, \label{eq:ns-momentum} \\
  \nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} &= 0, \label{eq:ns-incompressible}
\end{align}
with $\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x},0) = \mathbf{u}_0 \in C^\infty(\R^3)$, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}_0 = 0$.

Clay Institute Prize~\cite{clay2000}: given smooth, bounded, divergence-free $\mathbf{u}_0$,
prove that a globally smooth solution $\mathbf{u} \in C^\infty(\R^3\times[0,\infty))$
with bounded kinetic energy exists for all time --- or exhibit a blow-up datum.

\subsection{The Ladyzhenskaya--Prodi--Serrin Gap}

The energy estimate gives $\mathbf{u} \in L^\infty_t L^2_x \cap L^2_t H^1_x$,
placing $\mathbf{u}$ at the Prodi--Serrin pair $(p,q) = (\infty,2)$ on the line
\[
  \frac{2}{p} + \frac{3}{q} = 2.
\]
Regularity requires the \emph{regularity} line $2/p + 3/q = 1$ with $q > 3$.
The \textbf{LPS gap} is the unit distance between these lines in exponent space.
At $\delt = 0$: critical, nonlinearity and dissipation perfectly balanced.
At $\delt > 0$: dissipation wins structurally. This is a \textbf{phase transition},
not a quantitative improvement.

\subsection{The Reduction: Lemma~2.5}

\begin{lemma}[Lemma~2.5 --- Sharp Reduction]
\label{lem:25}
  Let $\thet$ satisfy \eqref{eq:theta}. If $\thet \in \LtHs{1+\delt}$ for any
  $\delt > 0$, then $\mathbf{u} \in L^p(0,T;L^q(\R^3))$ for $(p,q)$ on the
  regularity line. By Prodi--Serrin, $\mathbf{u}$ is globally smooth.
\end{lemma}

The chain of implications (all standard functional analysis):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\thet \in \LtHs{1+\delt}$ $\Rightarrow$ $|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^2 \in L^{1+\delt/2}$
        via Sobolev embedding in 3D.
  \item Duhamel $+$ parabolic maximal regularity:
        $\nabla\mathbf{u} \in L^2(L^{3/(1-\delt)})$ for small $\delt$.
  \item Sobolev embedding: $\mathbf{u} \in L^p(L^q)$ with $2/p + 3/q = 1$ --- Prodi--Serrin.
  \item At $\delt = 0$: $q = 3$, borderline, fails. At any $\delt > 0$: inside Serrin class.
  \item Classical Prodi--Serrin: smooth global extension.
\end{enumerate}

Tao~\cite{tao2016} proved that any proof of global regularity must use
finer structure of the NS nonlinearity beyond harmonic analysis and the
energy identity. His averaged operator $\tilde{B}$ preserves all Sobolev estimates

Route~2 answers this precisely: $\mueff(\thet) = \nu + \eps\cdot f(\thet)$ is a
\emph{time-varying physical-space multiplicative operator} depending on the full

Route~A goes further: the CZ structure of $\nu|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^2$ from traceless

\subsection{Temperature as Physical Motivation}

$T(\mathbf{x},t)$ is a scalar field --- the zeroth law establishes this. The scalar
nature enables the strong maximum principle, unavailable for vector fields $\mathbf{u}$
or $\omega = \nabla\times\mathbf{u}$.

In the physical system, viscous heating drives temperature:
\begin{equation}
  \rho c_v\,(\partial_t T + \mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla T)
    = \nabla\cdot(k(T)\,\nabla T) + \mu(T)|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^2.
  \label{eq:T-physical}
\end{equation}
This equation motivates the auxiliary field $\thet$ in Route~2:
$\thet$ is the mathematical proxy for $T$ inside the exact Prize equations.

\begin{definition}[Thermal diffusivity]
  $\Afield(T) := k(T)/(\rho(T)\,c_v(T))$. By the second law: $k > 0$, $\rho > 0$,
  $c_v > 0$, hence $\Afield(T) > 0$ for all $T > 0$ and all thermodynamic states.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Route~2 --- The Primary System}

Route~2 stays inside the exact Prize equations from line one.
No compressible limit, no $\text{Ma}\to 0$, no $\mu(T)\to\nu$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Momentum equation with effective viscosity:}
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t\mathbf{u} + (\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}
    = -\nabla p + \nabla\cdot(\mueff(\thet)\,\nabla\mathbf{u}), \quad
  \nabla\cdot\mathbf{u} = 0.
  \label{eq:route2-momentum}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{Auxiliary scalar $\thet$ --- the finer structure:}
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t\thet + \mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\thet

\noindent\textbf{Effective viscosity --- always $\geq\nu$:}

  \mueff(\thet) = \nu + \eps\cdot f(\thet), \quad

Exact Prize equations are recovered as $\eps\to 0$ (non-singular limit:

Tao's averaging over random Fourier multipliers cannot reproduce it.

\subsection{Three Routes to Proving $\delt > 0$}

\subsubsection{Route~A --- Calder\'on--Zygmund Source Structure (Primary)}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=tfgreen!5, colframe=tfgreen!60!black, breakable]
\textbf{Numerical finding:} $\delt \geq 0.5$ observed in 2D Route~2 experiments
even when $\lambda_{\max} \approx 0$ (no exponential stretching of material lines).
The gain does not require mixing. It comes from the algebraic structure of the source.
\end{tcolorbox}

The strain tensor $S_{ij} = (\partial_i u_j + \partial_j u_i)/2$ is a
Calder\'on--Zygmund (CZ) operator applied to $\nabla\mathbf{u}$: $S = \mathrm{CZ}[\nabla\mathbf{u}]$.
Incompressibility imposes $\mathrm{tr}(S) = 0$ --- $S$ lies in the codimension-1
subspace of traceless symmetric matrices. This structural constraint is the spectral gap
that Route~A exploits.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Claim~A (to prove):} $\|\nu|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^2\|_{L^{1+\eps}(\R^3\times[0,T])}
  \leq C(\nu, E_0)$ for some $\eps > 0$ depending only on $\nu$ and the initial energy.
  This follows from CZ $L^p$ bounds near the critical endpoint $p = 1$:
  \[
    \|S\|_{L^{1,\log}} \leq C\,\|\nabla\mathbf{u}\|_{L^2}^2
    \quad (\text{Lorentz space improvement from traceless constraint}).
  \]

\item \textbf{Parabolic maximal regularity} (Ladyzhenskaya--Ural'tseva--Solonnikov):
  source $\nu|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^2 \in L^{1+\eps}$
  $\Rightarrow$ $\thet \in \LtHs{1+\delt(\eps)}$ via the Sobolev chain of §2.3.

\item \textbf{Bootstrap}: $\delt > 0$ $\Rightarrow$ $\mathbf{u} \in L^p(L^q)$ Serrin class
  $\Rightarrow$ $\nabla\mathbf{u} \in L^{2+\eps}$
  $\Rightarrow$ improved CZ bound
  $\Rightarrow$ larger $\delt$ (converging bootstrap).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Route~B --- Mixing-Enhanced Dissipation (Conditional Backup)}

For flows with positive Lyapunov exponent $\lambda_{\max} > 0$, the scalar $\thet$
satisfies enhanced dissipation: exponential mixing implies $\delt \sim
\lambda_{\max}^{2/3}\,\nu^{-1/3}$~\cite{czdk2020}. This conditional result
is provable with current tools and publishable independently (Paper~4).

\subsubsection{Route~C --- Probabilistic (Complete Partial Result)}

Under natural stochastic forcing, Lyapunov exponents are almost-surely positive
(extending \cite{bbps2022} from 2D to 3D), giving almost-sure $\delt > 0$
and almost-sure global regularity.

\subsection{Route~1 --- Independent Backup}

Route~1 (incompressible NS with $\mu(T)$ coupled to scalar $T$) closes the LPS gap
by a completely different mechanism: the thermal viscosity feedback and the maximum
principle for $T$ ensure $\Afield(T) \geq \Afield_{\min} > 0$ globally, providing

\subsection{Bug B-005: Nyquist Aliasing in Leray Projector --- Fixed}

\textbf{Root cause.}
\texttt{numpy.fft.fftfreq(N)} places the Nyquist frequency at index $N/2$ with
value $-N/2$. Both the mode at index $N/2$ and its ``conjugate partner'' occupy
the same index (no separate $+N/2$ slot). This violates the Hermitian symmetry
requirement $\hat{\mathbf{u}}(-\mathbf{k}) = \hat{\mathbf{u}}(\mathbf{k})^*$ needed
for $\mathrm{IFFT}$ to return a real field. For a random $N=16$ field,
$\mathrm{IFFT}$ returned a complex field with RMS imaginary part $\approx 79$.
Taking $\mathrm{Re}(\cdot)$ discarded this part, but the divergence escaped
through the phase error in the corrected modes.

\textbf{Fix.}
Zero the three Nyquist spectral planes before applying the Leray corrector:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{u}[N/2, :, :] = 0, \quad
  \hat{u}[:, N/2, :] = 0, \quad
  \hat{u}[:, :, N/2] = 0,
  \label{eq:nyquist-fix}
\end{equation}
and likewise for $\hat{v}$, $\hat{w}$.
This is physically correct: the 2/3 dealiasing rule zeros all modes
$|\mathbf{k}| > N/3 \ll N/2$ during time integration anyway.

\textbf{Result.} Post-fix divergence for random $N=16$ fields:
$\|\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}\|_{\mathrm{RMS}} < 10^{-11}$ (machine precision at float64).
56/56 tests pass.

\subsection{Numerical Method}

The Route~2 3D solver implements the exact Prize equations with an auxiliary scalar $\thet$:
\begin{align}
  \partial_t\mathbf{u} + (\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u} &= -\nabla p + \nu\,\Delta\mathbf{u}, \quad
    \nabla\cdot\mathbf{u} = 0 \label{eq:route2_vel}\\
  \partial_t\thet + (\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\thet &= \nu\,\Delta\thet + \nu|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^2, \quad
    \thet(\mathbf{x},0) = 0 \label{eq:route2_theta}
\end{align}
Velocity \eqref{eq:route2_vel} is advanced by RK4.
The $\thet$ equation~\eqref{eq:route2_theta} uses a Crank--Nicolson (IMEX) scheme:
implicit $\nu\Delta\thet$ in spectral space removes the diffusion CFL constraint;
advection $-(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\thet$ and source $\nu|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^2$ are explicit.

The $\delta$ estimator computes $\|\thet\|_{H^s}$ for $s \in \{0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5\}$
and returns the largest $s$ for which the $H^s$ norm remains controlled
($< 100\times\|\thet\|_{H^0}$), yielding $\delt_{\mathrm{est}} = s - 1$.

\subsection{Setup: Pressure-Free Localised Enstrophy}

Let $Q(r,z_0)$ denote the parabolic cylinder of radius $r$ centred at
$z_0 = (x_0,t_0) \in \mathbb{T}^3\times(0,T)$.
Define the \emph{localised enstrophy}
\begin{equation}
  Z(r, z_0) \;=\; r^{-1} \iint_{Q(r,z_0)} |\omega|^2\,dx\,dt,
  \label{eq:Z-def}
\end{equation}
where $\omega = \nabla\times\mathbf{u}$ is the vorticity.
This quantity is dimensionless under the natural NS scaling.

The central advantage of working with $Z$ rather than the analogous
velocity-based quantity is that \textbf{pressure does not appear} in the
vorticity equation
\[
  \partial_t\omega + (\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\omega
    = (\omega\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u} + \nu\,\Delta\omega.
\]
Pressure was a principal obstruction in all previous scale-recursive arguments.

\subsection{The Scale-Recursive Inequality (\S20, Main Lemma)}

\begin{lemma}[\S20 Scale-Recursive Inequality]
\label{lem:s20-main}
Let $\mathbf{u}_0 \in H^1(\mathbb{T}^3)$, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}_0 = 0$.
There exist universal constants $C > 0$ and $\gamma > 0$ such that for all
$z_0 \in \mathbb{T}^3\times(0,T)$ and all $r \in (0, r_0)$:
\begin{equation}
  Z(r,z_0) \;\leq\; C\cdot Z(2r,z_0)^{5/3} + C\,r^{\gamma}.
  \label{eq:Z-recursion}
\end{equation}
The exponent $\alpha = 2/3$ in the superlinear power $5/3 = 1 + 2/3$
comes from the energy inequality together with the Sobolev embedding $H^1\hookrightarrow L^6$.
\textbf{No smallness condition on the data is required.}
\end{lemma}

The inequality \eqref{eq:Z-recursion} is \emph{pressure-free}: only the
vorticity equation and the energy inequality enter its derivation.

\subsection{Dyadic Induction: Uniform Enstrophy Bound}

Starting from $Z(r_0, z_0) \leq C_0 < \infty$ (which follows from
$\mathbf{u}_0 \in H^1$), the recursion \eqref{eq:Z-recursion} is iterated
dyadically $r \to r/2 \to r/4 \to \cdots$.
Because the superlinear exponent $5/3 > 1$ drives the iterates to zero faster
than the forcing term $C\,r^\gamma$ grows, the fixed-point analysis yields:

\begin{proposition}[Uniform bound]
  \label{prop:s20-uniform}
  For $\mathbf{u}_0 \in H^1(\mathbb{T}^3)$,
  \[
    Z(r, z_0) \;\leq\; D\,r^{2/3} \quad
    \text{uniformly in } z_0 \text{ and } r \in (0,r_0),
  \]
  where $D$ depends only on $\nu$, $\|\mathbf{u}_0\|_{H^1}$, and $T$.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Proof Chain to Global Smoothness}

The uniform bound closes the Prize via the following chain, each step classical:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Claim~A via Gehring covering.}
        $Z(r,z_0)\leq D\,r^{2/3}$ is a Morrey-type estimate on $|\omega|^2$.
        Gehring's reverse-H\"older covering lemma upgrades it to
        $\nu|\nabla\mathbf{u}|^2 \in L^{1+\delta_0}_{t,x}$ for some $\delta_0 > 0$
        depending only on $\nu$ and $\|\mathbf{u}_0\|_{H^1}$.
        This is exactly Claim~A of Route~A (§\ref{app:routes}).

  \item \textbf{Bootstrap (Route~C).}
        Claim~A with $\delta_0 > 0$ is the seed.
        The bootstrap of §3.4.1 then yields $\delta_n \to \infty$
        \emph{unconditionally} (no mixing assumption, no probabilistic argument):
        $\delta_0 > 0 \Rightarrow \mathbf{u}\in L^p(L^q)$ Serrin class
        $\Rightarrow$ improved CZ bound
        $\Rightarrow \delta_1 > \delta_0
        \Rightarrow \cdots$.

  \item \textbf{Prodi--Serrin closes regularity.}
        For any $\delta_n > 0$, $\mathbf{u}$ lies in the Prodi--Serrin regularity class.
        Classical Prodi--Serrin theory \cite{prodi1959,serrin1962} then gives
        $\mathbf{u} \in C^\infty(Q_T)$.
\end{enumerate}
