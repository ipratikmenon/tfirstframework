\chapter{Session Index}
\label{ch:sessions}

\begin{record}
\textbf{Source and method.} Compiled from \texttt{SESSION-LOG/} (41 files) and
the session table in \texttt{PROGRESS.md}, cross-checked against the proof
document and \texttt{results/results.db}. Where a summary and the document
disagree, the entry follows the document and says so.

\smallskip
\textbf{A counting fact worth stating once.} The sessions are numbered to
\Sn{48}, and there are \textbf{forty-seven} of them: \emph{there is no
\Sn{19}}. Neither \texttt{PROGRESS.md} nor \texttt{SESSION-LOG/} contains one,
and no artefact is attributed to it. The gap is why this book's title page and
its opening chapter both say forty-seven while the highest tag is \(48\). The
row is kept below, empty, rather than silently closed up.

\smallskip
A further tag is in use. \Sn{49} is the session that wrote
Chapters~\ref{ch:pincer}--\ref{ch:where-it-stands} and these appendices, and
that returned to primary sources for Chapter~\ref{ch:openai}. It has no
session log at the time of writing and appears as the last row.

\smallskip
Seven sessions have no dedicated log file --- \Sn{01}, \Sn{07}, \Sn{11},
\Sn{20}, \Sn{21}, \Sn{24} and \Sn{28} --- and are reconstructed from
\texttt{PROGRESS.md} alone. \Sn{39} carries \emph{two} logs, because two
independent tracks ran under it. Forty-one log files, forty sessions logged,
forty-seven sessions run.
\end{record}

Two cross-cutting counts, which overlap the partition above rather than
extending it. \textbf{Five} sessions retracted a prior claim of the
programme's own: \Sn{31}, \Sn{34}, \Sn{36}, \Sn{43}, \Sn{44}.
\textbf{One} session discharged a hypothesis the programme had been carrying:
\Sn{48}.

\begin{obsv}[The rhythm of the record]
\label{obs:a2-rhythm}
Three features of the index are worth naming.

\smallskip
\emph{The programme's productive period was seven days.}
\Sn{32}--\Sn{38} ran between 18 and 19 July 2026 --- the alignment pincer, the
Moser scheme, the a priori bound, both rungs, Gram negativity, the localized
Constantin--Fefferman derivation and the Regime-A impossibility. Everything
after \Sn{39} is verification, consolidation, or the removal of a route.

\smallskip
\emph{Corrections cluster immediately after the work they correct.}
\Sn{34} corrects \Sn{33}; \Sn{36} corrects \Sn{35}; \Sn{43} corrects \Sn{42};
\Sn{47} corrects \Sn{46}; \Sn{49} corrects \Sn{40}. The one exception is the
big one: \Sn{31} corrects \Sn{29}, and the interval is two and a half months,
during which an \texttt{arXiv} submission was being prepared.

\smallskip
\emph{Four of the last eight sessions produced impossibility proofs or
retractions.} That is not decay; it is what a programme looks like once its
map is accurate enough that routes can be ruled out rather than merely
abandoned.
\end{obsv}
